e-Boks and Digital Post explained: The Danish digital mailbox that runs your life

If you live in Denmark, e-Boks is where your entire relationship with the government happens. Here's how it works, how to access it, and what to do when you get lost.

If you've just moved to Denmark, one of the first things you'll hear is: "Check your e-Boks."

But what is e-Boks? Why is it legally binding? And what happens if you never open it?

Here's the complete guide.

What is e-Boks?

e-Boks is a digital mailbox — originally a private product, now used as Denmark's national Digital Post system for public authorities. Since 2014, every resident of Denmark over 15 with a CPR number is automatically enrolled (unless formally exempted for disability or lack of digital access).

Letters sent to your Digital Post are legally valid the moment they arrive — regardless of whether you read them.

Digital Post vs. e-Boks vs. mit.dk — what's the difference?

Confusingly, there are multiple names in use:

  • Digital Post — the official government-mandated inbox for authority letters
  • e-Boks — a private company that provides one of the two apps you can use to read Digital Post
  • mit.dk — the newer government-owned app that also shows Digital Post

Both apps show you the same letters — you just pick the one you prefer.

💡 TL;DR: Digital Post is the government service. e-Boks and mit.dk are the two "readers" you can use. Letters live in Digital Post; you view them via one of the apps.

How to log in

You need MitID (Denmark's national digital ID). Options:

  • e-Boks app (iOS / Android) — most popular
  • mit.dk app — official government alternative
  • borger.dk → "Se din post" — desktop
  • Web login at e-boks.com or mit.dk

What kinds of letters arrive?

Public authorities that use Digital Post include:

Sender Common letters
SKAT Tax statements, refunds, restskat
Kommune Housing benefits, kindergarten, healthcare
Politiet Fines, traffic violations
Udbetaling Danmark Pensions, family benefits, folkepension
SU-styrelsen Student grants
Sundhedsvæsnet Hospital summons, screening invitations
SIRI / Udlændingestyrelsen Immigration, permits

Private companies also send via e-Boks (banks, insurance, utility providers). Those are not legally the same as public Digital Post — but you'll see them all in the same app.

The 3-minute weekly routine every resident should have

Set aside 3 minutes once a week. Do this:

1. Open e-Boks / mit.dk

Enable push notifications so you never miss a new letter.

2. Sort by sender

Filter for public authorities (they have official logos and end in .dk).

3. Read titles first

If the title contains any of these Danish words, open the letter immediately: - Frist — deadline - Partshøring — authority wants your comments - Rykker — reminder / overdue - Afgørelse — decision - Restskat — tax you owe - Indkaldelse — summons

4. If in doubt, translate

Take a screenshot. Use Google Translate for a quick check — but be aware Danish legal/administrative terms often mistranslate. For accuracy, use KommuNemt which is trained on Danish official letters.

What happens if you never check Digital Post?

Short answer: Legal consequences pile up.

Real-world scenarios we've seen:

  • 🚨 Missed partshøring → Kommune decided you owed 40,000 kr in housing benefit refund with no chance to protest
  • 🚨 Missed traffic fine → Original 1,000 kr fine grew to 3,500 kr with collection fees, then sent to Gældsstyrelsen
  • 🚨 Missed hospital summons → Lost place in queue, needed to be re-referred by GP
  • 🚨 Missed rent increase notification → Landlord's rent hike became automatically valid after 6 weeks

Can you opt out?

Only in specific cases:

  • You're exempted (disability, lack of digital access, or long-term abroad)
  • You're under 15 (letters go to guardians)
  • You've moved abroad and formally deregistered

To apply for exemption, you must appear in person at your municipality's Borgerservice. It's not granted casually.

Digital Post for non-Danish speakers

Here's the harsh truth: letters arrive in Danish, always.

Even if you set English as your Digital Post preference (via Settings), it only changes the interface — not the letter content. Authorities are not obliged to translate.

Your options:

  1. Learn key Danish administrative terms (frist, afgørelse, partshøring)
  2. Use Google Translate — quick but imperfect
  3. Use KommuNemt — an app built specifically for Danish letters. Take a photo, get plain English with what to do and when

Quick summary

  • 📬 Digital Post = your legally binding inbox from Danish authorities
  • 📱 Access via e-Boks app, mit.dk app, or borger.dk
  • 🇩🇰 Almost all letters are in Danish
  • ⏰ Check weekly. Set push notifications. Never ignore frist
  • 🚨 Missed letters = missed deadlines = real financial consequences

Welcome to digital Denmark — the country runs on this system, so make it your friend 📮
