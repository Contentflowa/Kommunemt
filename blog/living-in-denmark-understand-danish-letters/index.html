Living in Denmark? How to understand your Danish official letters

Denmark's official mail system (e-Boks/Digital Post) can be a nightmare if you don't speak Danish. Here's how to handle it without missing deadlines.

Denmark ranks among the best countries in the world to live in — but if you don't read Danish yet, the official mail system can feel like a black box. Letters arrive in e-Boks (Digital Post) and they're almost always in Danish, even if you registered with an English preference.

Miss a deadline and it can cost you real money. Here's how to stay on top of it.

What is Digital Post / e-Boks?

Digital Post is Denmark's mandatory digital mailbox for communication with public authorities. Once you have a CPR number and a MitID, you're automatically enrolled — and letters sent to Digital Post are legally valid, even if you never open them.

You access it via: - The e-Boks app (iOS / Android) — most convenient - borger.dk → "Post fra det offentlige" - mit.dk — the newer official portal

💡 Important: Digital Post is different from private e-Boks (from banks, insurance, etc.). Both live in the same app, but public authority letters have legal weight.

Which letters do you actually get?

The most common senders you'll receive letters from:

  • SKAT — Tax authority (annual tax statements, corrections, refunds)
  • Kommune — Your municipality (benefits, housing, healthcare, resident registration)
  • Udlændingestyrelsen (SIRI) — Immigration authority (permits, extensions)
  • Styrelsen for Patientsikkerhed — Health authority
  • Digitaliseringsstyrelsen — Digital services (MitID renewals, etc.)
  • Politiet — Police (fines, traffic, etc.)

The 4 things you must find in every letter

Regardless of who sent it, look for these four elements:

1. The sender (who wrote it)

Usually top-left. If it says "Skattestyrelsen" or "Skatteforvaltningen", it's SKAT. If it says your local kommune's name, it's the municipality.

2. The subject / case type

Often in a bold heading. Common Danish terms: - Årsopgørelse — annual tax statement - Forskudsopgørelse — preliminary tax - Restskat — tax you owe - Afgørelse — decision (usually about a benefit or permit) - Partshøring — the authority wants your comments before deciding - Indkaldelse — a summons or appointment

3. The amount (if any)

Usually highlighted or in a table. Watch for the currency kr (DKK).

4. The deadline

The most important part. Watch for these Danish words: - Frist — deadline - Senest den [date] — no later than - Inden [date] — before - Betalingsfrist — payment deadline

Common expat pitfalls

❌ Assuming the letter is spam Danish letterheads look formal but plain. That government logo isn't marketing — it's a real authority.

❌ Waiting until you learn Danish Deadlines don't wait for your language learning journey.

❌ Not enrolling in Digital Post If you're a new resident, verify you're enrolled at borger.dk. Missing letters "because you didn't know" is not a valid defense.

❌ Ignoring e-Boks notifications Enable push notifications in the e-Boks app.

What to do when you receive a letter

  1. Take a photo or screenshot immediately
  2. Look for the frist (deadline) first — put it in your calendar
  3. Translate the content — you have options: - Google Translate (free, but often mistranslates legal terms) - KommuNemt (built specifically for Danish official letters — explains in plain English including what to do and when) - Ask a Danish friend or colleague
  4. Respond via MitID login on the relevant portal (skat.dk, borger.dk, or the specific authority)

Key portals every expat should bookmark

  • 🌐 borger.dk — Central citizen portal (English available)
  • 🌐 skat.dk — Tax authority (English available)
  • 🌐 lifeindenmark.dk — English guide to Danish public services
  • 🌐 nyidanmark.dk — Immigration (SIRI)

When you should get professional help

If the letter involves: - Deportation or residence-permit issues → Contact a Danish immigration lawyer immediately - Debt over 50,000 kr → Contact your union or a legal advisor (some offer free consultations) - Health-related with dates you can't attend → Call the sender to reschedule; don't just ignore

Quick summary

  • 📬 Digital Post is legally binding — read it weekly
  • ⏰ Deadlines (frist) are almost never negotiable
  • 🇩🇰 Almost all letters are in Danish, even if you set English preference
  • 📱 Use e-Boks app + notifications
